How the shop works

Buyers browse your catalog, loaded from a simple JSON file, and tap Buy on a product. The bot creates the order and shows your payment instructions — bank transfer, payment link, wallet, whatever you actually accept. The buyer pays, taps ‘I've paid’ and sends a photo of the payment proof. The photo lands in your admin chat with Approve and Reject buttons, and one tap notifies the buyer automatically. Buyers can check their own order history, and you get admin commands for orders and stats.

What is in the ZIP

The entire bot lives in one readable, commented Python file built on python-telegram-bot v21, with a catalog file you edit to change products, SQLite order storage that needs no database server, and an .env configuration for your token, admin ID, shop name, currency and payment instructions. A systemd unit runs it 24/7 on any small Linux server, and the included PDF guide walks through BotFather setup, installation, a complete first-order test, customisation, going live, troubleshooting by symptom and security practices.

Who it is for — and its honest limits

The kit suits sellers who take payment manually, digital-product sellers who deliver by hand, and anyone who wants a shop they fully own. Payment is manual by design: the bot collects proof photos and a human approves them; it does not process cards itself, and automatic file delivery is not built in — the guide shows exactly where it would go. You need Python 3.10+ and to be comfortable pasting commands into a terminal; you do not need to be a programmer to run the shop as it comes.
