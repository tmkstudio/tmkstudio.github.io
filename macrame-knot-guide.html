Almost every macramé piece you have ever admired is built from a surprisingly small vocabulary of knots — and most tangles in learning come from fuzzy diagrams. This macrame knot guide library teaches the twelve knots that cover almost everything: Lark's Head, Square Knot, Alternating Square Knot, Half Knot Spiral, Double Half Hitch (horizontal and diagonal), Clove Hitch, Gathering Knot, Overhand, Figure-Eight, Barrel Knot and Berry Knot. Each knot gets a clear cord diagram — the finished knot with the key passes drawn and the moving cord shaded — followed by numbered step-by-step instructions.

The guide also names the classic traps while you are still on the practice cord, not halfway through a wall hanging. Why does a square knot spiral when you repeat the same half twice? How do you keep a row of double half hitch travelling straight instead of sagging? Those small explanations are the difference between a reference you consult once and one that lives on your worktable.

Three projects to put the knots to work

Once the knots feel familiar, three complete geometric projects are drawn as knot maps with cutting lists: a Simple V wall hanging, a Diamond wall hanging, and a classic net plant hanger sized for 4–6 inch pots. Each project lists cord lengths to cut before you start, the finished size, and the knot sequence row by row. Cord-length rules of thumb are stated openly — working cords at roughly four times the finished length for square-knot work — and project lengths are clearly labelled as estimates, so you can adjust for your own tension.

Getting set up

The guide is written for 4 mm braided cotton cord, the forgiving all-rounder for wall hangings and plant hangers, and it suits beginner to intermediate makers. Print the knot pages and keep them in a folder by your dowel; macramé is a muscle-memory craft, and the fastest progress comes from tying each new knot ten times in scrap cord before it ever appears in a project. Within a week of short sessions, the square knot family will feel as automatic as tying your shoes.
