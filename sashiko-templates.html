Sashiko, the Japanese art of decorative running stitch, lives or dies by the accuracy of the pattern underneath the needle. These sashiko printable templates give you eight traditional geometric motifs drawn as clean dashed stitch lines at exact print scale: Seigaiha (blue ocean waves), Asanoha (hemp leaf star), Shippō (seven treasures), Kikkō (tortoise shell), Ichimatsu (checkerboard), Uroko (scales), Yabane (arrow feathers) and Kagome (woven bamboo lattice). Print a page, transfer the design to your fabric, and start stitching — no redrawing grids by hand, no guessing at proportions.

Every motif comes in two scales. The 6 × 6 inch tile suits cushions, tote panels and quilt blocks, where the geometry can breathe. The second sheet repeats the motif as a 2 × 2 grid of 3 inch tiles for all-over patterns on larger cloth. Each page carries a 1-inch test square, so you can confirm your printer is running at exactly 100% before you commit to fabric. A transfer guide explains the three reliable methods: lightbox tracing, carbon paper, and printing onto stick-and-stitch stabiliser.

Projects these templates are made for

Sashiko began as a way to strengthen and decorate working cloth, and these motifs still shine in that role. Use a single tile as a visible mending patch over worn denim, four tiles as a set of coasters, or a repeated sheet across the front of a tote bag. The geometric patterns also sit comfortably in modern quilting — a Kikkō block in white thread on indigo cotton makes a striking cushion with very little effort. If you can sew a running stitch, you already have every skill these templates require.

Stitching tips for clean sashiko lines

Load two or three stitches onto the needle before pulling it through; that rocking rhythm is what gives sashiko its even, flowing dashes. Keep the thread relaxed rather than tight — sashiko fabric should stay soft, not puckered. And work the long straight lines of a motif first, then the short connectors: the geometry settles into place and the final rows feel almost meditative.
