Counting tiny squares off a picture chart is where many beadwork projects slow to a crawl. These peyote bracelet patterns remove that friction entirely: alongside the colour chart for each design you get a complete word chart — every one of the 90 rows written out bead by bead — plus a legend with Miyuki Delica 11/0 codes and the exact number of beads needed in each colour. You can shop your bead stash before you thread a needle, and while you work you simply read the row, pick up the beads, and stitch.

The set includes eight even-count peyote designs: Diamond Medallion, Rainbow Chevron, Greek Key, Garden Vine, Sweet Hearts, Sun Medallion, Ocean Waves and Argyle. Each bracelet is 24 beads wide by 90 rows, which works out to roughly 1.5 × 6.75 inches (3.8 × 17 cm) before the clasp in Delica 11/0, and uses about 1,080 beads. The word-chart convention is explained on the guide page — rows 1 and 2 are strung together, odd rows read left to right and even rows right to left, matching the way the beads actually sit in the work.

New to peyote stitch? Start here

The PDF opens with an illustrated beginner guide to even-count peyote: how to string the first two rows, the zig-zag thread path that follows, and how to finish the ends ready for a clasp. Notes on lengthening and shortening the bracelets are included, and the charts can also be worked in brick stitch if that is your preference. The patterns are written for Miyuki Delica 11/0 cylinder beads; Toho Treasures 11/0 give an equally neat result.

Beading tips from the workbench

Keep a consistent, snug tension — peyote rewards it with flat, flexible fabric. If a row feels slow, work from the word chart and check your place against the colour chart every ten rows; the two views catch different mistakes. For the Rainbow Chevron and Ocean Waves designs, lay your bead colours out in order before starting each session, because the gradual colour shifts are easy to misread once you are in a rhythm.
