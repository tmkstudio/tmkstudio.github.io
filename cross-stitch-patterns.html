If you are looking for printable cross stitch patterns that you can start the same evening you download them, this bundle was made for you. The Woodland Garden Cross-Stitch Bundle collects twelve original counted charts in one PDF: Fox in the Garden, Owl on a Branch, Mushroom Cottage, Sunflower, Bee & Hive, Butterfly, Robin, Hedgehog, Acorn, Watering Can, Snail and Forest Moon. Every design is a small, satisfying project — between 40 and 52 stitches wide — so a single chart can be finished over a weekend and turned into a hoop, a card, a nursery piece or a patch.

Each chart is printed twice in the PDF: once as a full-colour chart and once as a high-contrast black-and-white symbol chart, so you can work from whichever version your eyes prefer. The colour key lists DMC thread codes together with the exact stitch count for every colour. That small detail saves real money and frustration, because you can check your existing floss stash before buying anything and know precisely how much of each skein a design will use. Finished sizes are given for 14, 16 and 18 count fabric, and the symbol charts import cleanly into pattern-tracking apps such as Pattern Keeper if you like to mark off stitches digitally.

Who these printable cross stitch patterns suit

The bundle is aimed at beginner to intermediate stitchers. All twelve designs use full cross stitches only — there is no backstitch and there are no fractional stitches to wrestle with — which makes the set a gentle next step after a first kit. A short how-to page at the front covers choosing fabric, hooping up, and starting and finishing threads neatly. Because each motif is self-contained, the charts also work beautifully as a stitch-along with a friend, as handmade gifts, or as stock for craft-fair hoops.

Tips for stitching the woodland set

Start with the Acorn or the Snail if you want a one-evening win; both use a small palette and read clearly even at 18 count. For the Fox in the Garden, stitch the fox first and the background foliage last, so the animal stays the focal point. If you plan to frame a piece in its hoop, wash and press it face-down on a towel before tightening the fabric — the stitches keep their loft and the finished piece looks noticeably more polished.
